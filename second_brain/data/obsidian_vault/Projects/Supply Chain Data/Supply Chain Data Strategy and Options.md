## Background and Assets

Over the past 18 months, extensive investigations into supply chains have uncovered evidence of forced labor implicating dozens of brands across 60 different cases. A unique methodology for identifying these issues has been developed, with potential for automation using artificial intelligence. Currently, the process is manual, but there is a pathway to automation. The data and methodology are highly valuable to brands and supply chain due diligence firms.

## Strategic Options

1. **Direct Approach to Brands**: Contact brands directly to inform them of risks in their supply chains, offering valuable insights to mitigate exposure under regulations like the UFLPA. This targets low-hanging fruit with immediate revenue potential.
2. **Partnership with Supply Chain Due Diligence Firms**: Collaborate with established firms to provide an additional layer of detailed evidence and value to their existing client base. This could involve sharing data or co-developing services, positioning us as a complementary rather than competitive entity.
3. **Third-Party Collaboration for Quick Revenue**: Work with a trusted third-party supply chain due diligence firm to approach clients on our behalf, sharing data and providing extra services for quick revenue generation and credibility with larger players.
4. **Sell Data Directly**: Sell the current dataset to a supply chain due diligence firm to raise immediate funds, potentially reinvesting into further development.
5. **Raise Investment**: Seek investment to build a comprehensive product, though this is less realistic currently due to the lack of a fully developed product. Options include partial equity investments or funding from supply chain firms.
6. **Partial Equity Investment**: As part of payment or collaboration, explore taking partial equity in partner firms as a long-term strategy.

## Constraints and Considerations

Key constraints include limited financial resources to expand operations or build a comprehensive product. While there is an AI engineer on the team, scaling would require a larger team. The uniqueness of the data and methodology must be protected to avoid direct competition with larger firms offering broader services, as large corporates prefer consolidated providers. The focus should be on carving a niche with a unique offering.
